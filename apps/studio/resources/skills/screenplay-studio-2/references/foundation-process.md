# Foundation Process (Studio 2)

The foundation process is the originality engine. By the time pages are written, distinctiveness is already won or lost. This document defines how foundations get built so the output is **specific rather than generic** — singular work that may invent freely OR deliberately evoke a chosen muse, but never defaults to the statistical average.

## Governing Principle

> **Convention earns the structure; specificity earns the distinction.**
> Obey the legacy skeleton that the top 100 screenplays share. Make every *choice inside that skeleton* specific — through invention or through deliberate evocation of a named source. The only enemy is the generic default that belongs to no one.

Two clarifications this principle settles:

1. **Originality is not the goal — specificity is.** Wanting a script to feel like *Heat*, hit like *Whiplash*, or move like *Drive* is a legitimate creative target, not a failure of imagination. Evoking a muse means *a specific, chosen lineage executed with your fingerprint*. Generic means *no source at all — the average of everything*. We reward the former and reject only the latter.

2. **Structure is enforced; structural subversion is a human-gated exception.** The skeleton (inciting incident, act turns, midpoint pivot, climax that resolves the dramatic question, scenes that turn) is enforced by default. Deviating from it — nonlinear time, withheld resolution, reverse order — is available only as an explicit human decision with a stated reason, never an AI default.

## The Spine

Everything derives from one idea. World-class scripts have a **central spine** where premise, theme, the protagonist's wound, the opposition, and the world are all views of a single thing. *Chinatown*: "you can't fix a corrupt system" *is* Gittes's wound *is* the water conspiracy *is* the noir shape. Competent-but-forgettable scripts have four sound elements that don't lock into one.

The Spine is authored first and gated first. Every subsequent foundation document must derive from it and prove the connection. If a character, structure, or world choice can't trace back to the Spine, it's either wrong or the Spine is incomplete.

## Stage Order

```
1. SPINE                  → The one idea. Authored and gated first.
2. CREATIVE INTENT + MUSE → Touchstones, the feeling chased, tone/style DNA.
3. PREMISE                → The dramatic situation that expresses the Spine.
4. THEME                  → The argument (with a credible opposing view).
5. OPPOSITION             → The antagonist/force, developed to win the argument.
6. CHARACTERS             → Four pillars + contradiction + specificity.
7. WORLD / TONE           → The specific world the Spine needs to pressure-test.
8. STRUCTURE              → The shape that is the only right shape for THIS.
9. SCENES                 → Scene Craft Specs; beats realized specifically.
10. COHERENCE & SPECIFICITY GATE → Holistic proof before export.
11. EXPORT                → Handoff package to the writer.
```

Two deliberate placements:

- **Style/Muse sits at the front (stage 2), not the end.** Style and tone are creative DNA, part of the muse. If the script evokes *Drive*, the style *is* the point and must shape character and scene work — not get bolted on as a finishing coat.
- **The gate is at the back, but it is not the only specificity check.** Each stage closes with a *light* specificity check (cheap, inline — see below). The final gate validates *coherence of the whole*, which can only be judged once all parts exist.

## Choice-Based Authoring (how every stage runs)

Foundation stages are **not** generate-then-approve. That makes the human a veto, and a veto can only reject the generic — it can't generate the singular. Instead, every stage is **choice-based**:

> At each stage, present **2-3 genuinely divergent options** — different *directions*, not variations on one idea — each internally coherent, each with its tradeoff named and (where useful) anchored to a muse. Give a recommendation. The human selects or redirects.

Example — Opposition stage:

> **Option A — Institutional (leans *Michael Clayton*):** the antagonist is a system; cold, procedural, no single villain. Tradeoff: harder to give a face and a climax.
> **Option B — Personal mirror (leans *Heat*):** a single antagonist who is the protagonist's equal and inverse. Tradeoff: risks the obvious doppelgänger beat unless made specific.
> **Option C — Internal (leans *Nightcrawler*):** the protagonist *is* the opposition; the force is their own appetite. Tradeoff: needs a strong external pressure so it doesn't go static.
> **Recommendation:** B, because the Spine is about [X], and a mirror antagonist dramatizes that argument most directly.

This is faster than blank-page authoring and more *yours* than rubber-stamping: taste expresses itself through selection among strong divergent options, the way a director works with a room. Options should be muse-aware where relevant, so the human is choosing the *feeling* at each fork.

**Human gates remain mandatory** — but they are now generative (the human picks a direction) rather than custodial (the human approves a proposal).

## Per-Stage Specificity Check (light, inline)

At the close of each stage, before moving on, run a fast check — not the full gate:

- **Name the obvious version.** What's the default, statistically-average way this element usually goes?
- **State our version and its source.** Are we beating the default through invention, or deliberately evoking a named muse? Either passes.
- **Fail condition:** the element is the generic default with no chosen source and no specific invention. If so, redirect before proceeding.

This keeps genericness cheap to catch (at the stage where it's easy to change) instead of expensive to discover (at the script layer).

## What "human input at every stage" now means

The human is the source of distinctiveness because distinctiveness comes from taste. The process pulls that taste forward:

1. **Seeds** — the Creative Intent capsule (touchstones, the obsession, the feeling chased, the bad version feared) is captured first and threaded into every stage's options.
2. **Selects** — at each stage the human chooses among divergent directions.
3. **Redirects** — any option can be rejected with a steer, generating a fresh set.

The AI proposes structured divergent choices; the human authors by choosing. That is how the foundation stays singular without asking the human to write from a blank page.
