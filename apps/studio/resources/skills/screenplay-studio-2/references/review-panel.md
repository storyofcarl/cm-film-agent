# Review Panel

An on-demand evaluative pass that reviews an artifact — outline, beat sheet, treatment, foundation package, or full draft — from three independent external perspectives: **the Critic, the Buyer, and the Audience.** Invoked at critical points by the user; not part of the automatic flow. Advisory and read-only: it reports, it never edits.

## Why this exists, and how it differs from the gates

The foundation gates (Coherence & Specificity) check the work from the *inside*: does it lock, is it specific, is it sound. The Review Panel checks it from the *outside*: how will it be received. These are different questions, and a script can pass one and fail the other — a flawlessly coherent, specific foundation can still be commercially unviable or emotionally inert.

The three perspectives are deliberately in tension. A script can be a Critic's darling and a Buyer's nightmare; a Buyer's slam dunk and an Audience's chore. Surfacing that tension is the value — it tells the user *which* kind of strength and weakness they're holding, so they can decide what to optimize for. The panel does not resolve the tension; the user does.

## When to invoke

The user calls it; it does not fire automatically. Natural invocation points:

- After the **outline** or **beat sheet** — cheapest, highest-leverage moment to learn the work is fundamentally a hard sell or emotionally flat.
- After the **treatment** or **full foundation package** — before committing to writing pages.
- After the **first full draft** — before a revision cycle.
- Before sending to a real reader, buyer, or competition.

It can review any artifact at any stage; it scales its depth to what it's given.

## The Three Perspectives

Each perspective is a distinct point of view with its own priorities, its own failure conditions, and its own voice. The panel runs all three independently — they do not coordinate or soften each other — then surfaces where they agree and where they collide.

### 1. The Critic
Cares about: craft, originality, thematic depth, character truth, directorial/authorial voice, how it sits against the canon and against current prestige work.

Evaluates:
- Is the premise fresh, or a familiar idea without a fresh angle?
- Does theme dramatize through choice, or get stated?
- Are characters specific and contradictory, or legible types?
- Is the structure earning its shape, or following a template?
- Does it have a voice — would a critic be able to name what's distinctive?
- Where does it sit relative to comparable acclaimed work? Does it add to the conversation?

Failure conditions the Critic flags: competence without distinction; theme-as-lecture; tidy resolution that forecloses meaning; derivative beats; the absence of a point of view.

Voice: rigorous, allergic to the generic, respects ambition, unimpressed by mechanics alone.

### 2. The Buyer
Cares about: market viability, comps, packageability, budget implications, audience size, castability, the logline's hook, where this sells and to whom.

Evaluates:
- What's the one-line hook, and is it a hook?
- What are the comps, and how did they perform? Is this a recognizable-but-fresh proposition or an unsellable orphan?
- Who's the audience and how big? Four-quadrant or narrow?
- What's the implied budget, and does the ceiling justify it?
- Are there roles that attract talent? A part an actor chases?
- What's the format/platform fit (theatrical, streaming, prestige limited, etc.)?
- Where does it sag commercially — second-act drag, an ending that won't satisfy, a premise that screens poorly in a logline?

Failure conditions the Buyer flags: no hook; no comps or only flops; budget/ambition mismatch; no role talent wants; an ending that tests poorly; "I don't know how to sell this."

Voice: pragmatic, comps-driven, not cynical but accountable to money and audience; will praise a clear commercial engine and name an unsellable premise plainly.

### 3. The Audience
Cares about: the felt experience — engagement, clarity, emotional payoff, momentum, satisfaction. Not craft in the abstract; *what it's like to watch.*

Evaluates:
- Where does attention wander? Where's the slow stretch?
- Is it clear what's happening and what's at stake, moment to moment?
- Do we care about the protagonist? When, and why?
- Does the emotional payoff land — do we *feel* the ending, not just understand it?
- Are the highs high enough? Is there a scene people will talk about?
- Does it deliver on the promise of its premise/genre (the implicit contract)?

Failure conditions the Audience flags: confusion, slow patches, low stakes, a protagonist we don't bond with, an ending that's correct but unmoving, a premise that over-promises and under-delivers.

Voice: honest about boredom and confusion, speaks in felt experience not craft terms, the proxy for the room.

## Output format

```
REVIEW PANEL — [artifact reviewed] — [stage]

THE CRITIC
  Verdict: [one line]
  Strengths: [2-4, specific]
  Concerns: [2-4, specific]
  Sharpest note: [the single most important thing]

THE BUYER
  Verdict: [one line]
  Hook / logline read: [...]
  Comps: [...]
  Strengths / Concerns: [...]
  Sharpest note: [...]

THE AUDIENCE
  Verdict: [one line]
  Engagement map: [where it grips / where it sags]
  Emotional payoff: [lands / falls short — where]
  Strengths / Concerns: [...]
  Sharpest note: [...]

WHERE THEY AGREE
  [Consensus strengths and problems — high-confidence signal]

WHERE THEY COLLIDE
  [The tensions — e.g. "Critic loves the ambiguous ending the
   Audience finds unsatisfying and the Buyer says won't test."
   This is the decision the user must make.]

THE ONE THING
  [If the user changes one thing, the panel's pick — noting which
   perspective it serves and which it may cost.]
```

## Rules of use

- **Advisory only.** The panel reports; it never edits the artifact, changes a foundation document, or auto-triggers a revision. The user decides what to act on.
- **Independent voices.** The three are run honestly and separately. The panel does not blend them into mush or soften the Buyer to spare the Critic. Disagreement is the product.
- **Honest, not cruel.** Accountability without abuse — specific, constructive, respects the work and the user's intent. (Consistent with the suite's feedback stance: critical thinking and honest assessment are never suppressed, but delivery stays constructive.)
- **No false consensus.** If all three genuinely like it, say so plainly — don't manufacture concerns to seem rigorous.
- **Scales to the artifact.** A beat sheet gets a structural-level read; a full draft gets scene-level engagement mapping. The panel says what it could and couldn't assess given what it was handed.

## Relationship to the rest of the suite

- **Distinct from the analyzer.** The analyzer is a craft *diagnostic* (metrics, structure, register, length). The Review Panel is *reception simulation* (will it land, sell, move people). Use the analyzer to fix the script; use the panel to decide if the script is worth fixing, and for whom.
- **Feeds choices, not gates.** Panel notes can prompt the user to revisit a foundation stage — but that's the user's call, made through the normal choice-based redirect, not an automatic loop.
- **Pairs with the Creative Intent capsule.** The panel can read the artifact against the capsule's stated intent and target feeling — e.g., "you wanted the *Whiplash* dread; the Audience read confirms it lands in Act 3 but not Act 2." This grounds reception against what the user was actually going for.
