## CHARACTER STATE CHANGE GUIDELINES

A character "state" represents a distinct emotional, psychological, or situational condition. State changes should be:

1. **Clearly Defined**: Each state should have identifiable characteristics
2. **Measurable**: The change should be observable through action or dialogue
3. **Meaningful**: Changes should impact the character's decisions or relationships
4. **Properly Paced**: Major characters need 3-5 significant state changes per act
5. **Motivation-Driven**: Changes should arise from character motivations or external pressures

## CHARACTER TRAITS TRACKING CODES

Create custom codes for each character's key traits and states. Examples include:

- **C+/-**: Confidence increasing/decreasing
- **V+/-**: Vulnerability increasing/decreasing
- **D+/-**: Deception increasing/decreasing
- **F+/-**: Fear increasing/decreasing
- **H+/-**: Hope increasing/decreasing
- **I+/-**: Isolation increasing/decreasing
- **R+/-**: Resolve increasing/decreasing

## STATE CHANGE DOCUMENTATION FORMAT

When documenting state changes, include:

1. The specific scenes where changes occur
2. Brief description of the catalyst for the change
3. Observable manifestations of the new state
4. Impact on other characters or plot progression
5. Connection to character's core motivation or fear

## CHARACTER FUNCTION TYPES

- **Protagonist**: Main character whose goals drive the story
- **Antagonist**: Character who opposes the protagonist's goals
- **Mentor**: Character who guides or teaches the protagonist
- **Ally**: Character who assists the protagonist
- **Threshold Guardian**: Character who presents challenges to protagonist's progress
- **Herald**: Character who announces change or challenge
- **Shapeshifter**: Character whose loyalty or identity is uncertain
- **Trickster**: Character who provides comic relief or disruption
- **Shadow**: Character who represents what protagonist could become (negatively)

## STUDIO 2 ADDITIONS — Distinctiveness Layer

The four-pillars work above ensures characters are *sound and consistent*. These additions ensure they are *singular*. Apply to every major character.

### CONTRADICTION (co-equal pillar)
The most memorable characters hold a genuine contradiction that doesn't fully resolve — not a flaw to be cured, but a tension they live in (Michael Corleone's loving ruthlessness; the Nightcrawler lead's affable predation). State the character's central contradiction and confirm it is *dramatized*, not merely noted. A character whose wound cleanly explains all behavior is legible but generic; the contradiction is what makes them feel like a person.

### SPECIFICITY PASS
The concrete, non-generic surface that belongs only to this person:
- **Speech texture:** rhythm, diction, what they never say (ties to voice bible).
- **Signature move:** the rhetorical/behavioral habit that's theirs alone (deflects with questions, over-precise, answers a different question).
- **Physical/behavioral specifics:** what they carry, do with their hands, the tell — observed, not typed.
- **Obliqueness:** how often they say the thing directly vs. around it (default everyone too direct = AI tell).

### SPECIFICITY CHECK
- Obvious version: what's the stock version of this role? (the gruff mentor, the loyal sidekick) Name it.
- Our version + source: beaten via invention or muse-evocation? Either passes.
- Fail: the legible type with no contradiction and no specific surface.

(For the antagonist, develop opposition to this same depth using `theme-and-opposition.md` — credible logic, a round they win, specific tells.)
