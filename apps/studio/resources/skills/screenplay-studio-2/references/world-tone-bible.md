# World / Tone Bible

A specific world with its own logic and texture — not a genre's stock furniture. This is the "specific style" half of the goal at the level of *place and feel*. Authored at stage 7, after characters (so the world can be built to pressure these specific people) and derived from the Spine.

## Why this exists

The generic trap operates at the world level too. "Thriller = rain-slicked city at night, blue grade" or "small-town drama = diner, water tower, church" are genre defaults — the average of all such films, belonging to none. World-class scripts have a *particular* world: its own rules, recurring images, sensory signature, and a tone it commits to (and tones it refuses). *Parasite*'s vertical geography, *Fargo*'s snow-flattened politeness, *Blade Runner*'s perpetual acid rain — the world is an argument, not a backdrop.

## The Bible

### 1. The world's specific logic
What is true *here* that isn't true everywhere? The rules — social, economic, physical, institutional — that this world runs on and that pressure the Spine.

> *Parasite*: wealth is literally vertical; you ascend or flood. Everything obeys this.

Derive from the Spine: the world should be the environment that pressures the spine's idea hardest. If the spine is about corruption being unfixable, the world's logic makes fixing it structurally impossible.

### 2. Sensory signature
The specific, recurring sensory texture — not a mood-board of genre clichés. What this world *sounds, smells, feels* like. Three to five concrete signatures, each specific enough to belong only to this film.

> Not "gritty urban." → "The constant low hum of freezer compressors. Fluorescent light that makes everyone look already dead. The smell of industrial bleach over something it isn't quite covering."

### 3. Recurring motifs and images
The visual/conceptual rhymes that will thread through the script. Ties directly into the echo map (in structure). 3-5 motifs with their meaning and how they progress.

> Hands (cleaning, taking, hiding) — appears in [acts]; progresses from tending to taking to being caught.

### 4. Tonal range — and the tones it refuses
The band the film lives in, stated as a range, plus the explicit exclusions. The refusals are as important as the inclusions (and connect to the capsule's anti-touchstones).

> **Lives in:** clinical dread with sudden tenderness; dry, withheld humor.
> **Refuses:** sentimentality, irony-as-distance, action-movie cool, quirk.

### 5. Touchstones for THIS world
2-3 references for the *world specifically* — which may differ from the story/character touchstones. Where does this world's feel come from?

> The institutional texture of *The Wire*'s Baltimore; the domestic uncanny of *Hereditary*'s house.

## Choice-based authoring

Offer **2-3 divergent world treatments** of the same premise, each with a different logic/tone, muse-anchored, with tradeoffs:

> **Option A — Naturalist (leans *The Wire*):** procedural realism, no stylization, the world is just *accurate*. Tradeoff: less heightened, harder to make iconic.
> **Option B — Heightened-clinical (leans *No Country*):** spare, controlled, slightly airless; the world feels designed. Tradeoff: can read cold.
> **Option C — Uncanny (leans *Hereditary*):** the familiar made wrong; domestic surfaces hiding dread. Tradeoff: tonal tightrope.
> **Recommendation:** [one], because [Spine derivation].

## Validation
Passes when: the world has a specific logic that pressures the Spine; the sensory signature is concrete and singular (couldn't be lifted into another film); motifs are defined with progression; the tonal range has explicit refusals; and it's anchored to specific touchstones rather than genre defaults.

## Per-stage specificity check
- **Obvious version:** what's the genre-default world for this story?
- **Our version + source:** invented or muse-evoked? Either passes.
- **Fail:** the genre's stock setting with no specific logic, signature, or refusals.

## Relationship to Style Bible
World/Tone governs *what the world is*; Style Bible governs *how the prose renders it*. The tonal range here sets constraints the Style Bible's register choices must serve — e.g., a world that "refuses sentimentality" forbids the prose from naming emotions directly.
