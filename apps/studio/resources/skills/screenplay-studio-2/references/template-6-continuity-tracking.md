## CONTINUITY TRACKING GUIDELINES

### When to Update
- After completing each screenplay chunk (typically 20-25 pages)
- Before beginning work on the next chunk
- When making significant revisions to existing content

### Tracking Focus Areas
1. **Character Progression**: Monitor emotional states, relationships, and motivations
2. **Narrative Elements**: Track setups, payoffs, questions, and revelations
3. **Structural Balance**: Assess scene functions, tension variation, and pacing
4. **Genre Elements**: Track genre-specific requirements and conventions

### Continuity Types to Monitor

#### Physical Continuity
- Character appearances, injuries, possessions
- Environmental details and settings
- Time progression and day/night cycles

#### Character Continuity
- Personality traits and behavioral patterns
- Knowledge (what they know and when they learned it)
- Skills and abilities
- Speech patterns and vocabulary

#### Plot Continuity
- Cause and effect relationships
- Information revelation timeline
- Setups and corresponding payoffs
- Core conflict progression

### Common Continuity Issues to Watch For

1. **Character Knowledge Errors**: Characters knowing information they haven't learned yet
2. **Motivation Inconsistencies**: Characters acting against established drives without explanation
3. **Timeline Problems**: Events occurring out of logical sequence
4. **Dropped Setups**: Introducing elements that are never paid off
5. **Premature Payoffs**: Delivering payoffs for setups that weren't properly established
6. **Tone Shifts**: Sudden changes in tone without proper transition or motivation

### Using the Tracking Document Effectively

1. **Be Specific**: Include scene numbers and concrete details
2. **Be Comprehensive**: Track all major narrative elements
3. **Be Proactive**: Identify potential issues before they become problems
4. **Be Solution-Oriented**: Suggest specific fixes for identified issues
5. **Be Forward-Looking**: Plan upcoming elements to maintain narrative momentum
