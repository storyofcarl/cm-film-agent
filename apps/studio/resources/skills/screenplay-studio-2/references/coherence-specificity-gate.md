# Coherence & Specificity Gate

The final foundation validation, run after scenes and before export. Light specificity checks happen at every stage; this gate validates the **whole** — the thing that can only be judged once all parts exist: do the elements lock into one singular, coherent work, and is that work specific rather than generic across its entirety?

Nothing exports to the writer until the foundation passes this gate. Originality and coherence are cheap to fix here and expensive to fix at the script layer.

## Why a holistic gate (not just per-stage checks)

Per-stage checks catch a generic *element*. They can't catch a foundation where every element is individually fine but the *whole* is incoherent or generic — the four-competent-but-separate-elements failure. Coherence is an emergent property; it's only visible with all parts on the table. This gate is where the Spine's promise (five views of one idea) gets verified in practice.

## The gate has two tests. The foundation must pass both.

### Test 1 — Coherence (does it lock?)

Verify the Spine actually runs through everything:

- **Spine traceability:** premise, theme, opposition, each major character's wound, world logic, and structure each demonstrably derive from the Spine. Not "compatible with" — *expressions of*.
- **Theme ↔ opposition lock:** the opposition embodies a credible counter-argument to the theme and wins at least one round.
- **Want/need ↔ climax lock:** the climax forces the protagonist's want/need choice, and that choice dramatizes the theme.
- **World ↔ Spine lock:** the world is the environment that pressures the Spine hardest, not a neutral backdrop.
- **Echo/motif coherence:** motifs from the World/Tone Bible have setup and progression across the structure.
- **No orphan elements:** every character, subplot, and setup serves the Spine or earns its place; flag anything that's merely "good" but disconnected.

**Pass:** the foundation reads as one inevitable idea expressed many ways.
**Fail:** competent elements that don't reinforce each other → revisit the weakest links (human-gated).

### Test 2 — Specificity (is it ours, not generic?)

Across the whole foundation, applied as a set:

- **Premise specificity:** name the generic version of this premise; confirm ours is beaten via invention or muse-evocation.
- **Character specificity:** each major character has contradiction and concrete non-generic surface — not a clean legible type.
- **Opposition specificity:** specific logic and tells, not a stock villain.
- **World specificity:** singular sensory signature and refusals, not genre furniture.
- **Scene-level spot check:** sample pivotal scenes; confirm turns, surface/underneath gaps, and anchor details are present and specific.
- **Bad-version-feared check:** hold the whole foundation against the Creative Intent capsule's named failure mode. Does any part drift toward it? Fix before export.
- **Muse alignment (not imitation):** where the work evokes touchstones, confirm it's *evocation with our fingerprint*, not pastiche. Evoking *Heat*'s respect-between-adversaries register = pass. Copying its bank-heist beat = fail.

**Pass:** specific throughout — every major choice belongs to this film, by invention or chosen lineage.
**Fail:** generic defaults survive somewhere → name them, beat them (human-gated).

## What specificity does NOT require

Per the governing principle, this gate does **not** reward novelty for its own sake, and does **not** penalize deliberate evocation of a muse. A beat that intentionally chases the *Whiplash* feeling passes specificity — it has a chosen, specific source. The only failure is the generic default that belongs to no one. The gate also does **not** subvert structure: a foundation that obeys the legacy skeleton passes coherence; structural deviation is only valid as a recorded, human-gated creative decision with a stated reason.

## Gate output

A short report, human-reviewed:

```
COHERENCE: PASS / FAIL
  - Spine traceability: [pass/issues]
  - Theme↔opposition lock: [pass/issues]
  - Want/need↔climax lock: [pass/issues]
  - World↔Spine lock: [pass/issues]
  - Orphan elements: [none / list]

SPECIFICITY: PASS / FAIL
  - Premise / character / opposition / world: [pass/issues]
  - Scene spot-check: [pass/issues]
  - Bad-version-feared drift: [none / where]
  - Muse alignment (evocation not pastiche): [pass/issues]

VERDICT: EXPORT-READY / REVISIT [stages], gated by human
```

## Relationship to the script-layer audit

This gate validates the *foundation*. The richness/length/register audits (analyzer) validate the *script*. They are deliberately redundant on specificity — catching genericness twice (cheaply at foundation, again at script) is the design, because the foundation gate can't perfectly predict execution. But a foundation that fails this gate should never reach the writer.
