# Numbering Hierarchy

The full `SQ###-SC###-SH###` system. SKILL.md carries the one-paragraph summary; this is the depth and the worked examples.

## Contents
- SQ — sequence
- SC — scene
- SH — shot
- Acts vs. sequences
- When to start a new sequence / scene / shot
- Worked structure example

## SQ### — sequence (001–999)

Sequences are narrative units — complete story beats with a beginning, middle, and end (a chase, a heist, an investigation). A feature has ~8–15 sequences, multiple per act. Start a new sequence for: distinct narrative beats; complete dramatic units with a clear goal and resolution; major turning points/revelations; flashback or dream sequences with their own arc; montages that tell a unified story; time jumps creating a new unit; shifts in visual style signaling a distinct story mode. Think of sequences as chapters.

## SC### — scene (001–999)

Scenes are location/time-based groupings within a sequence. **Scene numbers reset to 001 at each new sequence.** Start a new scene for: each distinct location; each continuous unit of action in one place/time; location changes; time cuts within the same day; shot-reverse-shot coverage resets (new conversation location). Continue the same scene for coverage of one location, shot-reverse-shot within one conversation, different angles of continuous action. Think of scenes as "INT. WAREHOUSE — DAY."

## SH### — shot (001–999)

Shots are individual camera setups within a scene. **Shot numbers reset to 001 at each new scene.** Each new camera position, angle, or significant framing change is a new shot (establishing wide, medium on entry, CU, over-shoulder, POV, insert).

## Acts vs. sequences

- **Acts** are conceptual containers (Setup / Confrontation / Resolution) — NOT numbered. Typically three; Act 1 ~20–30 min, Act 2 ~40–60 min, Act 3 ~20–30 min.
- **Sequences** are the numbered units within acts (SQ001, SQ002…). Act 1 might hold SQ001–SQ003; Act 2 SQ004–SQ009; Act 3 SQ010–SQ012.

## When to start a new sequence

Distinct story units (chase, heist, training, investigation); narrative turning points; flashbacks with a full arc; montages; complete mini-stories with setup/action/resolution; major location-based story units; shifts in story mode (investigation → action, present → dream).

## When to start a new scene

Every location change; time cuts ("later that day"); each distinct event in one place; traditional script scene breaks (INT./EXT., DAY/NIGHT).

## When to start a new shot

Every camera-position change; every significant framing change; shot-reverse-shot (each angle); coverage variations (wide/medium/close of one action); inserts and cutaways.

## Worked structure example

```
ACT 1: SETUP (conceptual, not numbered)
  SEQUENCE 001 — Opening World
    SC001 Hero's apartment, morning (5 shots)   SQ001-SC001-SH001 … SH005
    SC002 Commute (3 shots)                      SQ001-SC002-SH001 … SH003
  SEQUENCE 002 — Meet the Problem
    SC001 Office discovery (6 shots)             SQ002-SC001-SH001 … SH006
ACT 2: CONFRONTATION
  SEQUENCE 003 — Entering the New World
    SC001 Warehouse investigation (8 shots)      SQ003-SC001-SH001 … SH008
    SC002 Warehouse exterior (4 shots)           SQ003-SC002-SH001 … SH004
  SEQUENCE 004 — Flashback to Origin
    SC001 Childhood home, past (5 shots)         SQ004-SC001-SH001 … SH005
ACT 3: RESOLUTION
  SEQUENCE 006 — Final Confrontation
    SC001 Showdown (10 shots)                    SQ006-SC001-SH001 … SH010
```

Acts are conceptual divisions; sequences are numbered narrative units within them; scenes are locations; shots are setups. Number sequentially with no gaps at any level.
